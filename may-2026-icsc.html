Retail Investment Is Back. Here's What the Numbers Are Telling Us.

Ryan Lundgren May 25, 2026 4 min read

The commercial real estate industry wrapped up ICSC Las Vegas this week, the sector's largest annual gathering, and the headline coming off the floor is straightforward: institutional capital has returned to retail in a meaningful way, and the transaction data backs it up.

For owners of retail investment properties, here is what is worth paying attention to.

The transaction numbers are the real story.

Research presented at the conference showed Q1 2026 retail investment volume hit $15 billion, the highest quarterly figure in three years. Institutional investors now represent 24% of multi-tenant retail sales on a year-over-year basis, their highest share in nearly a decade. That is not exploratory capital feeling out the market. That is conviction from some of the most disciplined buyers in commercial real estate.

When institutional money returns to a sector at that volume, it typically compresses cap rates and raises the floor on valuations across property types, including single-tenant and NNN assets. Owners trying to understand where their asset is priced today should be paying attention to who is buying and at what scale.

The leasing environment is providing the foundation.

The investment activity does not exist in a vacuum. Industry research presented at ICSC showed every major REIT is having their best leasing year ever, driven by years of limited new construction that has kept quality retail space genuinely scarce. That supply constraint is what gives buyers confidence underwriting retail deals right now. Vacancy risk looks different when there simply is not much new product coming to market.

The tenants leading leasing demand are restaurants, discount stores, and grocers. Specialty retail continues to contract. That bifurcation matters for investment sales because it is increasingly reflected in how buyers are underwriting credit and pricing deals. Necessity-based, service-oriented tenants in strong locations are where buyer appetite is most active.

What this means if you are a retail property owner.

The combination of record institutional participation, strong leasing fundamentals, and limited new supply creates a pricing environment that favors well-located assets with quality tenants. Buyers are in the market with capital to deploy, and the data coming out of this week's conference suggests the window of strong demand is not going away in the near term.

If you have been on the fence about understanding what your asset is worth in today's market, the activity level heading into the second half of 2026 makes it a worthwhile conversation to have now.
